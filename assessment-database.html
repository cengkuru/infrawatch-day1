The assessment database · what "ready" means

The database is the audit trail, not the score sheet

In this programme, the database is the assessment's system of record: every score together with the evidence that produced it. It is ready for assessment when it can preserve that trail, not merely produce a score. A score without its record will be hard to defend, correct, or hand over.

Is your database ready?

One test. Pick any scored data point and ask: could a reviewer who was not in the room see what was scored, from which source, found when, by whom, and why any exception was allowed? If the answer is yes for every point, the database is ready. If the database can only show the score, it is a results sheet, not yet the assessment database.

The minimum record, per project and per data point

Whatever the tool, a spreadsheet, a form system or a purpose-built platform, it must retain these six things. The structure is free; the retention is not.

1Project and delivery-model classificationWhat the project is and how it is delivered: its financing form, its classification, and whether it met the PPP definition. Classification decides which questions apply, so it must be stored, not remembered.
2Lifecycle stage, and applicability or evidence state for each pointWhich stage the project is at, and for every data point whether it was scored, not yet due, not applicable, or a searched-and-absent zero. The evidence states are what keep an empty cell from being a guess.
3Source URL, publisher, publication date and access dateWhere each fact was found, who controls that publication, when it was published and when you saw it. Web pages change and links die; the access date is what lets a score survive its source.
4Assessor and reviewerWho scored the point and who checked it. Accountability is per cell, not per country.
5Rationale for exceptionsWhy an aged project was admitted, why a threshold was applied, why funder-only evidence was recorded, and any point flagged for a ruling. An exception with its reason stands in a defensible list; an exception without one is a hole.
6Core versus buffer status, and case-study linkageWhether each project is in the core sample or drawn from the buffer list, including what it replaced and why, and which projects feed a case study.
The trap: building for the output instead of the trail. A tool that computes project results beautifully but stores only the scores fails this definition. Every correction then becomes an argument nobody can settle, every challenge to a score lands on someone's memory, and every handover, to a reviewer, to CIPE, to your own team next year, starts from scratch.

What the record is for: defending, correcting, handing over

  • Defend: the assessment's claims are only as strong as their sources. When a government or funder disputes a score, the answer is the recorded URL and access date, not a recollection.
  • Correct: scores will change, through review, through a conflicting-figures ruling, or through a source reappearing. A correction is safe only when the record shows what the score was based on before.
  • Hand over: assessors change, and the project result must be recomputable by whoever comes next. If the trail lives in one person's browser history, the assessment leaves with them.

Where this connects

The database records the outputs of the whole method: the selection documentation enters it, every score carries its search record and evidence state, and the 40 data points define its columns. The scoring rules live on those pages; this page only fixes what must survive in storage.
