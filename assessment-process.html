The assessment · end to end

The assessment scores what a government has made public, one fact at a time

A transparency assessment takes a list of eligible projects and, for each one, checks fact by fact whether the government published it and how easy it is to reach. It never judges whether the project is good; it measures the public record. The output is a scored profile per project and a documented trail behind every score.

The method in three sentences.
  • Select eligible projects fairly, and lock the list before scoring.
  • For each project, search the government's channels for each of the 40 data points, record the evidence state, and score it 0 to 6 with its source trail.
  • Review, then average the applicable points into the project result.

The process map

Three stages. Preparation finishes before scoring begins; the middle loop repeats for every applicable data point on every selected project; review can send a record back before results are computed.

The two ways an assessment becomes systematically misleading:
  • Never choose a project because its disclosure is easy to find. The sample then flatters the system it was meant to test.
  • Never score zero without a completed, recorded search. The score then measures your effort, not the government's disclosure.
Every other mistake can be corrected from the trail; these two poison the data itself.

Who and when: every scored point carries a named assessor and, where reviewed, a named reviewer; review happens before anything is reported. Timelines and review cadence are for the methodology guide to set.

What comes out the other end

  • A documented sample of projects
  • 40 scored data points per project, plus PPP answers where they apply
  • Flags on doubtful points
  • A per-project result
  • A defensible record of how all of it was reached

Selected projects can then be taken deeper as case studies.
