Financing and ownership record · who stands behind the project

Who put money into the project, through which entity, and who ultimately stands behind it

Also called the attribution record in the mapping report. Eligibility is two yes-or-no questions: public infrastructure function, and material foreign financial support. The third element is not a question at all. For every project that passes, one financing record is built, and everything the funder comparison later claims stands on it.

Five fields, for every financing party

For each financing party, not just the largest.

FieldWhat it captures
IdentityThe financier's legal name, from a source, never inferred. A consortium is recorded as its members, not collapsed into its best-known name: one Kenyan entry is being revised for exactly that simplification.
DomicileWhere the financing party is resident. Government borrowing from a lender resident in the same country is not foreign funding, however the facility is structured.
Ultimate controlWho ultimately controls the financing party, traced through holding companies. Where control is inferred from public records rather than stated, the inference and its basis are recorded, never silently upgraded to fact.
InstrumentThe financing type: equity, debt, grant, guarantee or another instrument, with amount, currency and share where published, each figure carrying publisher, scope and date.
Contractor originRecorded separately from financier origin. The builder's flag never decides classification, but it is kept, because the two axes answer different questions.

Never a gate: the record feeds classification and comparison

  1. It is not a gate. Inclusion is decided by the two questions in the eligibility test; the record never keeps a project out.
  2. It feeds classification. The PRC and non-PRC label is computed from the record: the largest documented financing share decides, and mixed or multilateral cases are visible because every financier is in the record.
  3. It makes the comparison answerable. The programme compares disclosure across funder categories. Without identity, domicile and ultimate control per financier, that comparison would rest on assumption; with them, it rests on disclosure.
  4. It keeps the two axes apart. Nepal's airport pair holds financing roughly constant while the contractor is Chinese in both cases. Only a record that carries both axes lets the analysis say which one it is testing.

One record, worked through

The DR Congo hydropower rehabilitation, from the report's diagnostic classifications, is the hard case the record is built for.

  1. The parties: a foreign mining group and its engineering contractor, jointly with the state utility as offtaker: an institutional joint venture, provisionally a PPP under the working test.
  2. Identity and instrument: each participant recorded by legal name and role; the financing recorded as it is structured, not as a notional loan.
  3. Ultimate control: the lead investor's ultimate control was inferred from public records, not stated in the submission, so the record says so: the inference, its basis, and its status as inference.
  4. Contractor origin: the engineering contractor is recorded on its own axis, leaving the financier comparison clean.
  5. Where the trail thins, the record shows where it thins: a resource-backed facility may name no cash lender, and the record then holds the facility, the parties and the valuation basis if published, rather than an invented amount.
The common trap: recording only the headline financier. The comparison is between funder categories, so a project with a Chinese equity stake, a multilateral guarantee and a domestic loan belongs to all three conversations; collapsing it to one lender makes the analysis simpler and wrong. Record every financing party, and let the classification rules do the deciding.
