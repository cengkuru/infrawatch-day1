Case studies · proposed working definition

A case study turns assessment results into explanation and action

A case study is a document built on one assessed project, or a deliberately matched pair, that explains why the disclosure record looks the way it does and names what a specific authority could publish to change it. The scores tell you what was published; the case study asks why: who controls the information, who gains from keeping it hidden, what law or budget rule stands in the way, and what one change would improve the record.

A case study begins after scoring. It does not replace the assessment, reopen project selection, or override the recorded evidence. Every claim it makes must trace back to scored points, sources and evidence states.

How it differs from the assessment

The assessmentThe case study
Scores every eligible project against the same questionsGoes deeper into one instructive project, or a matched comparison
Asks: what has the government made public?Asks: why does this record look like this, and what could improve it?
Produces comparable scoresProduces an evidence-based explanation and one practical next action

A case study has five parts

1The questionWhat pattern in the scored record needs explaining? An instructive gap, a strong contrast, a pattern other countries will recognise.
2The evidenceThe scores, source gaps, evidence states and flags that establish the pattern, drawn from the assessment database.
3The explanationWhich authority owns publication, who benefits from opacity or disclosure, and which fiscal, legal or political rules shape the record.
4The verificationWhich claims are proven against primary-source evidence, such as the contract, loan agreement, audit or official gazette, and which remain provisional and are labelled so.
5The actionWhat should be published, by which named authority, and which scores it would move. A case study without an owner and an action is unfinished.

The development process proposed, for CIPE confirmation

The trap: the case study drifting free of the assessment. If its claims cannot be traced back to scored points, sources and evidence states, it is opinion with a project name attached; and if it contradicts the scored record, one of the two is wrong, and the database is where that gets settled, not the prose. Until a case study is verified, it is a teaching sample, and it says so.
Status: draft v0.1, August 2026, for partner consultation. The definition and process on this page are a proposal for CIPE confirmation; the explanatory layer draws on the political-economy framing from the June 2026 Africa workshop. Case-study linkage is one of the six retention fields in the assessment database; early, unverified material sits in the findings ledger on its way to case-study grade.
