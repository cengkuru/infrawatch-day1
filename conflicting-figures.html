Conflicting figures · when official numbers disagree

You do not pick the true number. You keep both, and credit that the government published

Two official sources will sometimes give two different costs, lenders or classifications for the same project. The assessment is not an audit: its job is to record what the government disclosed, so a conflict between disclosures is itself a finding, and the score never depends on guessing which number is right.

The sequence

Proposed for every conflict between government sources; the wording awaits CIPE confirmation.

  1. Retain both values, each with its scope. A total project cost and a phase cost are often both correct; the scope line is what shows it. Every figure already carries its publisher, scope and date under the sourcing rule.
  2. Credit the act of disclosure. The government published; the score reflects that under the scale. A conflict never converts published information into a zero.
  3. Take the working value from the mandated project owner or direct line agency. Analysis needs one number to compute with; the responsible authority's figure is that number, without declaring the other wrong.
  4. Flag the unresolved divergence. The flag sits beside the score and travels with the project; flags never change scores.
Never do these: silently average the two numbers, silently drop one, or lower a score because the sources disagree. Each of those hides the very thing the record exists to show. And where the conflict is between a government figure and a third-party figure, there is no tie to break: the government figure is the disclosure, the third-party figure is evidence beside it.
