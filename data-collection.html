Data collection · guidance, not law

Collect the public record so every score can show its source

In this programme, data collection means systematically gathering what the government has published about each sampled project, point by point, keeping the source trail as you go. It is the search work that sits between selecting the sample and scoring it.

There is no single national process. Portals, registers and habits differ by country, so this page is guidance on a defensible collection cycle, not a prescribed procedure.

The collection cycle: five steps per project

Run per project, across its 40 data points. The order matters less than the habit: capture the trail at the moment of finding, not at the end.

1
Map the channels before you search

List the government channels where each kind of record should live in your country: ministry and agency sites, procurement and budget portals, debt databases, audit institutions, gazettes, SOE and regulator registers. This map is what makes a later sufficient search demonstrable, and it is reusable across all projects in the sample.

2
Search government sources first, point by point

For each data point, work through the mapped channels before anything else. The scale's turning point is official disclosure, so the government's own record is the primary object of collection, not a fallback.

3
Capture the trail at the moment of finding

For every find: source URL, publisher, publication date and your access date, straight into the assessment database. Save a copy or screenshot where the page looks unstable. Web pages change and links die; an uncaptured find may be gone by review time.

4
Then widen to non-government sources, labelled as such

Funder pages, contractors, media and research fill in what exists about the project, but they are recorded as what they are: non-official sources that cap a score at 2. Keep both figures when official and non-official sources disagree, and flag the divergence.

5
Close each point with a state, not a blank

Every data point ends the cycle in a named condition: found (and where), not yet due, not applicable, or searched-and-absent, using the evidence states. A blank cell is unfinished collection, not a zero.

Habits that keep the record defensible

  • Collection is not scoring. Gather first, score after. Deciding the score while hunting for evidence invites the record to fit the number.
  • Date everything, including your own visit. The access date is what lets a score survive its source disappearing.
  • Search in the country's languages. English and the host-country languages both count on the scale; a record is not absent because it was only published in one of them.
  • An absence is a result. A completed search that finds nothing is a finding worth exactly as much as a find, provided the search itself was recorded.
  • Do not let ease steer coverage. Give the hard points the same completed search as the easy ones; a record collected only where collection was pleasant reproduces the availability bias inside the project.
The trap: collecting the project instead of the record. The job is not to learn everything about the road; it is to establish, fact by fact, what the government has made public about it. Hours spent perfecting background knowledge that no data point asks for is collection effort the assessment cannot use.

Where this connects

Collection runs inside the assessment process, between selection and scoring. Its search standard is defined by sufficient search, its outputs land in the assessment database, and what it finds is scored on the 0 to 6 scale.
