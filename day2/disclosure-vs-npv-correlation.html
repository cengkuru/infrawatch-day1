Disclosure makes value testable.

Disclosure does not make a deal good. It determines whether the deal can be tested at all. Using CIPE's InfraWatch methodology, which maps to the CoST IDS for disclosure and adds PEA and NPV, this probe plots how much each project disclosed against whether its value can even be assessed. The slope you will see is mostly funder type, not transparency, and naming that is the finding.

Read this before you read the chart. This probe applies CIPE's InfraWatch methodology: a transparency assessment that maps to the CoST IDS data points, run alongside PEA and the NPV toolkit. It is a teaching and hypothesis artifact, not an empirical finding. Every number here is coded or estimated by the analyst from reported inputs (AidData, press, the Day-2 case studies), not pulled from a completed InfraWatch assessment or from loan-agreement-grade cash flows.
  • Disclosure axis = the InfraWatch transparency assessment, which maps to the CoST IDS data points. Here it is the analyst's coding of how many of those data points are evidenced in the reported record (0 = nothing public, 100 = full disclosure). It is not a completed InfraWatch assessment or an ITI Dimension-4 score.
  • Value-signal axis is two different measurements, and the chart marks which is which. A circle is an NPV computed with the InfraWatch / IEA-Kenya 16-step toolkit (discount rate = govt long-term bond at signing; social value capped 15%) on disclosed financing terms. A triangle is only an outcome-based verdict: where terms are confidential the toolkit cannot run, so the verdict is inferred from what the project did, not what it cost. Do not read a triangle as a computed NPV.
  • The confound is built in. NPV needs disclosed financing terms to compute. So a low-disclosure project literally cannot have a trustworthy NPV. Part of any upward slope is mechanical, not causal. n=10 cannot separate mechanism from funder-type confound from genuine effect.

Two things the shapes tell you that the slope hides

Half these "verdicts" are not the same measurement. The circles (Azura, Sankofa, Bui) are NPV computed from terms that were actually published. The triangles (SGR, Addis, GERD) are not NPV at all; they are outcome-based verdicts, because the financing terms were never disclosed and the toolkit could not be run. The slope plots both under one label. The shapes are there so you do not mistake "we judged the outcome" for "we computed the value."

The strongest evidence the relationship is not "transparency causes value" is Sankofa: a circle in the medium-disclosure band sitting deep in the harmful zone. Its terms were transparent enough to compute the take-or-pay poison, so disclosure caught the problem; it did not prevent it. Disclosure made the bad deal testable. It did not make it good.

02The off-diagonal cases are the lesson

A correlation coefficient on ten self-coded points would be statistical theatre. The defensible reading is to narrate the projects that break the expected pattern.

Visible · looks worthwhile (NPV computed)

Azura-Edo IPP

Open competitive procurement, World Bank/MIGA + IFC + 16 disclosed lenders. The "expected" diagonal, but it is the funder-discipline confound made visible, not proof transparency created the value.

Visible · still a bad deal (NPV computed)

Sankofa gas

The case that breaks the thesis. Terms were disclosed (~$250m/yr take-or-pay), so the NPV could be computed and it came out deeply negative. Disclosure surfaced a poisoned chalice it could not stop. Transparency is diagnostic, not curative.

Hidden · failure leaked out (outcome verdict)

Addis LRT · SGR

A white elephant (8 of 41 trains running) and a railway under documented debt-service strain (the SGR loan is roughly 5% of Kenya's GDP). No full NPV exists for either, the terms are confidential; we know they failed only because the outcomes leaked out.

Hidden · cannot be tested at all

NASENI solar

The honest blank cell. Financing terms never published on any official document, so NPV cannot be computed and there is not even an outcome track record yet. The opacity is itself the finding: you cannot assess what is not disclosed.

04What you can honestly say in the room

Not "transparency correlates with value, r = 0.x." A coefficient on ten analyst-coded points would be statistical theatre. Say this instead, and notice the headline claim is about assessability, not prediction:

Three claims this artifact supports

1. Disclosure is the binding constraint on assessment, not on value. The clearest pattern is not that opaque projects are bad, it is that opaque projects (NASENI) cannot be judged at all. You cannot run NPV on terms you do not have. That is the case for InfraWatch's disclosure leg in one sentence: the CoST IDS data points are what make the NPV toolkit usable.

2. The apparent disclosure-to-value slope is mostly funder type. PRC/commercial money clusters low-disclosure; multilateral money clusters high-disclosure-and-better-terms. Transparency rides along with funder discipline; isolating its own effect needs a far larger, funder-matched sample.

3. Transparency diagnoses, it does not cure. Sankofa proves disclosure surfaces a poisoned chalice without preventing it. The value of disclosure is that it lets NPV register the poison, exactly Kwame's ruling that the limit is disclosure, not the math.

05What would make this empirical

If you want a real correlation rather than a teaching artifact, the path is:

Step 1

Pull genuine IDS scores

Use the Ghana OC4IDS portal (STMA, 28 projects with real disclosure data) rather than coding headline megaprojects by hand. Small municipal works, but genuinely measured.

Step 2

Funder-match the sample

Hold funder type fixed (e.g. all PRC, varying disclosure) to break the confound. Only then does residual disclosure variation mean anything.

Step 3

Compute NPV on agreements

Discount = govt bond rate at signing, real cash flows from loan agreements, social value capped 15%. Mark computed vs coded so the gap stays visible.

Methodology: InfraWatch, developed by CIPE (Center for International Private Enterprise), with the NPV toolkit led by IEA-Kenya.
