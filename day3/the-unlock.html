This is the Standard Gauge Railway, near Voi. Start with the real thing, not the abstraction. The train is real, the viaduct is real, and so is the person walking beneath it.

The Day 3 finding, in one breath

Scoring and NPV measure the project. Disclosure decides whether the people living with it get a say. A sealed contract has no public. A published one hands the affected community the one thing it was missing: the standing to be heard.

Where the obligation has to land

The foreign funder

Lender confidentiality clauses are a frequent reason terms stay sealed. The ask is framed generically: foreign funders, not one country.

The private contractor

The party building the project holds terms the public never sees. Disclosure has to reach the contractor, not stop at the state.

Not just the region

Asking only regional or state actors to disclose leaves the two parties who actually hold the confidential terms untouched.

Where this comes from, and what is still to confirm

Source. InfraWatch Nairobi, Day 3 (4 Jun 2026), the CIPE partner day. Built from the day's themes, not a verbatim transcript or deck. A conceptual reading of the argument; it carries no figures.

The argument. The technical legs of the method (a transparency assessment that maps to the CoST IDS, political-economy analysis, and net present value) produce findings. What turns findings into political pressure is the public's voice, and what unlocks that voice is contract disclosure, aimed at foreign funders and private contractors. Affected populations are both a cost to be counted and a source of that voice.
