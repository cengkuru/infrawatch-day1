A community lives beside a project it never agreed to. The road, the dam, the mine arrives. They carry the cost. But the contract that decided their fate sits in a drawer marked confidential. They cannot argue with a document they are not allowed to read.

A sealed contract has no public.

On the last day, the method's real power came into focus. InfraWatch is not only scoring and net-present-value math. Its distinctive power is to gather and synthesise the voice of the public. And one thing unlocks that voice: a contract that is made public.

an agreement the public cannot see

start here

The agreement is real. The public is not in it.

The contract decides who pays, who benefits, and who bears the cost. While it is sealed, the people living with the consequences are not a party to it. They are a line item, not a voice.

the unlock

Publish the contract, and the seal breaks.

This is the switch. The moment the terms are public, the agreement can be read, argued with, and challenged. Disclosure is not paperwork. It is the entry condition for everything that follows.

the power

Now the public has a voice.

InfraWatch's distinctive power is to gather and synthesise that voice into the evidence. It bites hardest where communities are directly impacted. The affected population is not just a cost to count. It is a source of accountability.

aim it right

The obligation is on the funder and the contractor.

The duty to disclose belongs to the foreign funder and the private contractor, not only the host state. That is where confidentiality clauses hide the terms, so that is where the obligation has to bite.

The Day 3 finding, in one breath

Scoring and NPV measure the project. Disclosure decides whether the people living with it get a say. A sealed contract has no public. A published one hands the affected community the one thing it was missing: the standing to be heard.

01Where the obligation has to land

A common reflex is to demand transparency from the region or the host government. Day 3 sharpened it: that is not where the terms go dark.

The foreign funder

Lender confidentiality clauses are a frequent reason terms stay sealed. The disclosure ask is framed generically, foreign funders, not one country.

The private contractor

The party building the project holds terms the public never sees. Disclosure has to reach the contractor, not stop at the state.

Not just the region

Asking only regional or state actors to disclose leaves the two parties who actually hold the confidential terms untouched.
