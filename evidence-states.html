Evidence states · the label before every score

Before you score zero, answer one question: why is it missing?

A zero can mean four different things, and only one of them is a disclosure failure. The evidence state is the label recorded before any score, so a government is never penalised for a record that should not yet exist, and an empty cell never has to be guessed at.

The four kinds of zero

Every empty field lands in exactly one of these states before anything is scored. The state decides whether a score exists at all, and whether the point enters the project result.

Due but unpublished · recorded 0-D

The record should exist, and a completed search found nothing

The point applies, the project's stage has already produced it, and a sufficient search of the government's channels came back empty. This is the only state that is a disclosure failure.

Scores 0 · counts in the average
Verified not yet due · recorded N/A-T

The project's stage has not produced this record yet

A completion audit for a project still under construction cannot exist. The point is excluded and the expected stage or date is recorded, so it returns to the checklist when it falls due.

No score · leaves the average
Verified non-applicable · recorded N/A-M

The record can never exist for this project

A resettlement plan for a project with no resettlement, or a tender record a delivery model lawfully never produces. The reason and its evidence are recorded; the point is excluded.

No score · leaves the average
Insufficiently searched · no label yet

Nobody has finished looking

An unsearched point is not a zero. Until the search meets the sufficient-search standard, the field carries no state and no score: the only correct action is to keep looking, or record why the search is complete.

No score yet · keep searching
The common trap: treating every empty cell as a zero. In the early diagnostic material, the Cambodia and DR Congo submissions both contain zeros that are not disclosure failures; the DRC case study is pre-construction, so its completion documents cannot exist yet. Headline scores built without these labels punish honest reporting, and the cross-country comparison stops being publishable.

Two neighbours that are never zeros

Two further states sit beside the four, and both are answers, not absences.

The caseThe treatment
An official statement that there is nothing to disclose: "no households were displaced"A complete, scoreable disclosure. Never a zero.
Evidence published only by a funder or another third partyEvidence, labelled separately, capped at 2 on the scale. Never government disclosure.

The path an assessor walks

For every data point, the applicability and search checks come first. A no at any of them produces a label, never a zero.

  1. Does this point apply to this kind of project? No: record N/A-M with the reason and evidence. The point leaves the calculation.
  2. Is it due at the project's current stage? No: record N/A-T with the expected stage or date. The point leaves the calculation.
  3. Has a sufficient search been completed? No: no state, no score. Search the channels in the country's source inventory and record the search itself.
  4. Did the search find a qualifying government publication? Yes: apply the 0 to 6 scale and record source, publisher, scope and date. No: record 0-D and retain the search record.

One point, worked through

The contract-variation reason, from the mapping report's worked examples, shows how demanding a real zero is.

  1. Official evidence shows a variation occurred, so the reason is a record this project owes. The point applies.
  2. The variation is signed and in effect, so the reason is already due.
  3. The assessor completes a sufficient search of the procurement portal, the ministry pages and the published contract records, and records the search.
  4. No published reason is found. Only now does the field score zero, recorded 0-D, with the search record retained as its evidence.
Open with CIPE, CoST and Verité: Verité Research proposes an "unverified applicability" label for points where official sources cannot establish whether a trigger or stage has occurred, and its note places "cannot be determined from official sources" under "not yet applicable", so the two proposed labels overlap. Until the overlap is resolved in Phase 2, a point whose applicability cannot be determined is returned for clarification: it takes no score and is not counted, pending referral. Neither proposed label affects scoring today.
