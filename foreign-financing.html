Foreign financing · the shared definition

Whether a project is foreign-financed depends on who is asking. Here, everyone asks the same question.

Every country's laws test foreign involvement differently, so the same project can be foreign-financed in one country's records and invisible in another's. A comparison across countries only works if the programme asks one question everywhere.

The problem the definition solves

Take one railway: built with a loan from a foreign state bank, no foreign owner, repaid as public debt. Run it through three common legal tests and you get three different answers. None of the tests is wrong. They were written to protect different things.

The shared definition

The programme does not replace any country's legal test. It adds one question that every partner asks the same way, so twenty projects in one country can be compared with twenty in another.

Working definition · draft · one wording for every country

A project is foreign-financed when it receives material financial support from at least one identifiable foreign source, through a loan, an equity or ownership stake, a guarantee, or another financial instrument, and the support is signed and documented.

Material means the support funds the asset itself, not studies or promotion around it; as a working reading, support whose withdrawal would not stall or shrink the asset is not material, and any numeric threshold is for the methodology guide to set. Identifiable means the financier can be named from a source, and traced through holding companies to its origin. Signed means announced intentions do not count; a signed arrangement counts whether or not funds have yet moved, and a guarantee counts from the moment it is signed, because money at risk is support.

What the words mean in practice

Two boundary lines settle most arguments: what makes a source foreign, and what makes support financing.

Foreign means the source, traced to its origin
  • Foreign means foreign to the country where the asset is built. The origin of the money decides: not the currency it is paid in, not the nationality of the builder, and not where the equipment comes from.
  • Trace through holding companies. A stake held through a third-country vehicle counts by its ultimate owner, not by the vehicle's registration. Where a named financier's trail cannot be traced further, keep the project and flag it; opacity is itself a finding. A project with no identifiable foreign source at all never enters.
  • State-owned and private both count. A state bank, a development agency, a company, or a fund: what matters is that it is foreign and identifiable.
  • Two governments building a shared project each fund their own side; each side is domestic to its own country and foreign to the other's records.
Financing means money at risk or owed, so these do not count
  • Construction contracts. Being paid to build is revenue, not financing. The builder risks nothing after payment.
  • Equipment supply, consultancy and design work. Services sold to a project, for the same reason.
  • Feasibility studies and preparatory grants. Money around the project, not in the asset. A grant that materially funds the asset itself is different: it counts, recorded by name.
  • Announcements, intentions and unsigned agreements. Until signed, there is nothing to document and nothing at risk.

How the definition is used

  1. It is question two of the three-question eligibility test. A project with no qualifying foreign financing does not enter the dataset.
  2. Once a project qualifies, record the financing form, and where several foreign sources qualify, record them all. The form decides which questions apply and which record to ask for first.
  3. Record the country's own legal answer alongside the shared one. Where the two disagree, the gap is itself a finding: an obligation the country's records were never built to see.
  4. Cross-border projects: the unit is the project as it appears in the assessing country's list, scored against that country's records. A shared project may sit in both countries' lists, each side scored on its own records, and comparing the two is part of the point.
  5. The workbench, built in an earlier phase, practises the neighbouring step: given the financing, which records to demand. It does not test this definition; eligibility is settled here first.
