The 40 data points · what gets scored

The questions follow the project's life, so the gaps show where the record stops

Every project is scored on the same forty facts, ordered along the project's life from the first identification papers to the final audit. That ordering is what makes a missing answer informative: for each fact that applies to a project, a gap tells you at which stage the public record went dark. The forty come from the CoST Infrastructure Data Standard and are published in open-data form as OC4IDS, the open-data standard for infrastructure projects.

Forty facts across five phases

The width of each block is its share of the forty. Tender management carries the most questions because contracting is where the most money changes hands on the most decisions.

Identification7 Preparation7 Tender management14 Implementation6 Completion6

Where to expect the thinning: a common pattern in public records is that the trail runs strongest up to contract award and weakest afterwards. The two amber phases mark that territory: their points exist precisely because that is where changes to price, scope and time happen, and where disclosure most often stops. A project still earlier in its life does not score these phases as zero; their points are simply not yet due. Each applicable point is scored on its own and carries equal weight in the project result, which is the sum of the applicable scores divided by the applicable count, so a phase with more points carries more of the average. The forty apply to every project; projects that pass the PPP definition answer an additional set.

One standard, one count, two table shapes: the CoST Data Publication Manual sets the canonical figure at 40 proactive points, 20 project-level and 20 contract-level. A worksheet showing 39 rows is the same standard, not a different one: the OC4IDS core mapping presents the contract-level points as procurement and implementation groups and combines contract start date and contract period into a single row. Cite 40 as the total; treat any 39-row sheet as a presentation difference, never a missing point.

The points, phase by phase

The workflow: select the points that apply, score each on the 0 to 6 scale, record flags beside doubtful evidence, then read where the sequence breaks. Names as they appear in the assessment score sheet; plain readings added where two points are easy to confuse. Each is scored separately on the 0 to 6 scale.

Phase 1 · Project identification

What is this project, and whose is it?

  1. 1Project reference number
  2. 2Project owner
  3. 3Sector, subsector
  4. 4Project name
  5. 5Project location
  6. 6Purpose
  7. 7Project description

If these are missing, the project cannot even be identified in the public record, and nothing downstream can be checked against anything.

Phase 2 · Project preparation

What will it take, and who pays?

  1. 8Project scope (main output)
  2. 9Environmental and social impact
  3. 10Land and settlement impact
  4. 11Contact detailsthe postal and email address of the project owner
  5. 12Funding sources
  6. 13Project budget
  7. 14Project budget approval date

Funding sources is where this library's foreign financing and financing form records attach.

Phase 3 · Tender management

Who got the contract, how, and for how much?

  1. 15Procuring entity
  2. 16Procuring entity contact detailsthe same, for the organisation running the tender
  3. 17Procurement process
  4. 18Number of firms tendering
  5. 19Cost estimatethe owner's pre-tender estimate, before any bids
  6. 20Contract type
  7. 21Contract administration entitywho administers the contract, where different from the procurer
  8. 22Contract title
  9. 23Contract firm(s)
  10. 24Contract pricethe price at contract award
  11. 25Contract scope of work
  12. 26Contract start date
  13. 27Contract duration
  14. 28Contract status (current)pre-award, active or closed

Fourteen of the forty points sit here. A direct award does not empty this phase: the method, the chosen firm, the legal basis and the price stay applicable and scoreable. And a project delivered through several awarded contracts is scored per awarded contract, per the recorded ruling.

Phase 4 · Implementation

What changed while building, and why?

  1. 29Variation to contract pricechanges to the price, excluding inflation adjustments
  2. 30Escalation of contract priceinflation and currency adjustments under the contract's own clauses
  3. 31Variation to contract duration
  4. 32Variation to contract scope
  5. 33Reasons for price changes
  6. 34Reasons for scope & duration changes

Changes are normal in construction. Undisclosed changes are the finding: every point in this phase asks what moved and whether the public was told why.

Phase 5 · Completion

What did it finally cost, and who checked?

  1. 35Project status (current)which lifecycle stage the whole project is in now
  2. 36Completion cost
  3. 37Completion date
  4. 38Scope at completionwhat was actually delivered, against the original scope
  5. 39Reasons for project changesthe completion-stage summary of all change: scope, time and cost together
  6. 40Reference to audit and evaluation reportslinks to published technical and financial audits

The comparison the whole record builds toward: what was promised at award against what was delivered at completion, and whether anyone audited the difference.

How the forty are used

  • Each point is scored separately on the 0 to 6 scale, producing a disclosure profile and a project result; the project result page carries the equal-weight average and its worked example.
  • Only applicable points count. A point that is not yet due at the project's stage, or does not apply to its procurement form, leaves the calculation rather than scoring zero. Due-ness is judged point by point, never by dropping whole phases: mid-construction, the completion records are not yet due while the projected completion date already is. The point-by-point guidance lives in the methodology guide.
  • PPP projects answer more. A project that passes the PPP definition is additionally assessed on the PPP questions, covering the obligations the forty do not reach: guarantees, risk allocation, tariffs, renegotiations. How those questions bind to the forty is being settled in the methodology note.
