The 40 data points · what gets scored

The questions follow the project's life, so the gaps show where the record stops

Every project is scored on the same forty facts, drawn from the CoST Infrastructure Data Standard and published in open-data form as OC4IDS. They are ordered along the project's life, from the first identification papers to the final audit, which is what makes a missing answer informative: it tells you at which stage the public record went dark.

Forty facts across five phases

The width of each block is its share of the forty. Tender management carries the most questions because contracting is where the most money changes hands on the most decisions.

Identification7

Completion6

Where to expect the thinning: in most public records the trail is strongest up to contract award and weakest afterwards. The implementation and completion points exist precisely because that is where changes to price, scope and time happen, and where disclosure most often stops.

The points, phase by phase

Names as they appear in the assessment score sheet. Each is scored separately on the 0 to 6 scale.

Phase 1 · Project identification

What is this project, and whose is it?

  1. 1Project reference number
  2. 2Project owner
  3. 3Sector, subsector
  4. 4Project name
  5. 5Project location
  6. 6Purpose
  7. 7Project description

If these are missing, the project cannot even be identified in the public record, and nothing downstream can be checked against anything.

Phase 2 · Project preparation

What will it take, and who pays?

  1. 8Project scope (main output)
  2. 9Environmental and social impact
  3. 10Land and settlement impact
  4. 11Contact details
  5. 12Funding sources
  6. 13Project budget
  7. 14Project budget approval date

Funding sources is where this library's foreign financing and financing form records attach.

Phase 3 · Tender management

Who got the contract, how, and for how much?

  1. 15Procuring entity
  2. 16Procuring entity contact details
  3. 17Procurement process
  4. 18Number of firms tendering
  5. 19Cost estimate
  6. 20Contract type
  7. 21Contract administration entity
  8. 22Contract title
  9. 23Contract firm(s)
  10. 24Contract price
  11. 25Contract scope of work
  12. 26Contract start date
  13. 27Contract duration
  14. 28Contract status (current)

Fourteen of the forty points sit here. A direct award does not empty this phase: the method, the chosen firm, the legal basis and the price stay applicable and scoreable.

Phase 4 · Implementation

What changed while building, and why?

  1. 29Variation to contract price
  2. 30Escalation of contract price
  3. 31Variation to contract duration
  4. 32Variation to contract scope
  5. 33Reasons for price changes
  6. 34Reasons for scope & duration changes

Changes are normal in construction. Undisclosed changes are the finding: every point in this phase asks what moved and whether the public was told why.

Phase 5 · Completion

What did it finally cost, and who checked?

  1. 35Project status (current)
  2. 36Completion cost
  3. 37Completion date
  4. 38Scope at completion
  5. 39Reasons for project changes
  6. 40Reference to audit and evaluation reports

The comparison the whole record builds toward: what was promised at award against what was delivered at completion, and whether anyone audited the difference.

How the forty are used

  • Each point is scored separately on the 0 to 6 scale, so a project produces a disclosure profile, not a single grade.
  • Only applicable points count. A point that is not yet due at the project's stage, or does not apply to its procurement form, leaves the calculation rather than scoring zero.
  • PPP projects answer more. A project that passes the PPP definition is additionally assessed on the PPP questions, covering the obligations the forty do not reach: guarantees, risk allocation, tariffs, renegotiations.
