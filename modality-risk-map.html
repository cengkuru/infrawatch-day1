Day 2 · reading a project by its money

Every funding structure has a blind spot.

Two projects can look identical from the outside and hide completely different things, because they were financed in different ways. The structure tells you, in advance, which fact will disappear.

scroll to see each one ↓

five structures, five blind spots

Same outside.
Different secret.

Scroll through five flagship projects. Each one looks like any other piece of infrastructure, until you see how it was financed.

Sovereign loan · a national railway

The headline is the line.
The real debt is not.

The state borrows to build. The project gets announced; the liability it loads onto the treasury stays out of view.

Ask only for the announced cost and the debt stays dark. A sticker price is not a loan book.

Sovereign loan · what brings it back

One record turns the lights on.

Publish the loan terms and the guarantee, and the real debt appears: the interest, the repayment, the contingent liability.

the record: loan terms + state guarantee

Equity stake · a power plant

You can see the price.
Not who gains.

An investor takes a share. You can know exactly what was paid and still not know who sits behind the stake and captures the value.

Ask only for the contract price and the beneficiary stays dark. Price and ownership are different questions.

Equity stake · what brings it back

One record turns the lights on.

Publish the beneficial owners, and you see who is really behind it, and who the value flows to.

the record: beneficial ownership

PPP / concession · a toll road

A budget shows this year.
The bill runs 25 years.

A private partner builds and operates. The guarantees, availability payments and contingent liabilities run for decades.

Ask only for the project budget and the future bill stays dark. This year is not the whole story.

PPP / concession · what brings it back

One record turns the lights on.

Publish the contract and its fiscal commitments, and the long-term bill to the public becomes visible.

the record: contract + fiscal commitments

EPC + finance · a container port

One total.
Nothing to check it against.

Build and finance are rolled into one bundled deal. The mark-up hides inside a single number with nothing to compare it to.

Ask only for the total value and the mark-up stays dark. A lump sum cannot be benchmarked.

EPC + finance · what brings it back

One record turns the lights on.

Publish the itemised cost breakdown, and the price can finally be benchmarked, line by line.

the record: cost breakdown + benchmark price

Grant / aid · a water system

The money looks free.
The strings do not show.

A funder gives, with terms. The conditions attached, and any requirement to buy from the funder's own firms, decide who really benefits.

Ask only for the grant amount and the conditions stay dark. Free money is rarely free of terms.

Grant / aid · what brings it back

One record turns the lights on.

Publish the source, its conditions and any tied procurement, and the real terms show.

the record: source + conditions + tied procurement

Five structures. Five blind spots.
Five records that reveal them.

The point, in one breath

A funding structure is a prediction engine. It tells you which fact will disappear before you read a single document, and the one record that brings it back into the light.

→The disclosure triage card

The whole story on one card. See the funding structure, ask for the record that reveals its blind spot. Screenshot it and take it into a real request.
