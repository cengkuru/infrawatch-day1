InfraWatch Nairobi · Day 1 · the morning round

Five partners sat around one table: Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria, Ethiopia, DRC. Each named a different priority. It sounded like five separate agendas.

Five accents, one sentence.

Watch the local words fall away. Underneath the variation, every partner was making the same demand, in the same three words.

The surface · five asks, as they were said

the demand that was already in the room

Do not ask people to trust what they cannot see.

The surface

They sounded different.

Kenya wanted the appraisal before the build. Ghana, the owner of the resource. Nigeria, the numbers. Ethiopia, the quality of the investment. DRC, the delivery. Five partners, five priorities, in their own words. Ethiopia and DRC are lower-confidence readings, still to be confirmed.

The split

Every ask had the same opening.

Look at where each sentence begins. The first two words never change: Show us. Only the tail does. The stem is shared; the tail is the local variation.

The strip

Take the local words away.

Strip each tail and the same sentence is left five times over. Not five agendas, one demand wearing five accents: do not ask people to trust what they cannot see.

The lanes

The tails are real, and they each have a tool.

Shared demand does not erase local nuance. Each tail becomes an action lane: the ask, the evidence to publish, and the one number that would prove it. The tool already exists, no new method required.

The common ground

They did not ask for the same project. They asked for the same right: show us enough to judge.

Receipts: how we read the overlap

Filled dot = the partner holds that priority. Hollow dot (◌) = lower-confidence reading. Columns sorted by partners-per-priority.

How we read this, and what stays uncertain

What this is. A working read of the Day-1 morning session in Nairobi, 2 June 2026, not a completed partner survey. Each partner's ask is captured in their own framing; the priorities grid is an analyst's reading of which themes each emphasised.

What stays uncertain. Ethiopia and DRC are tagged lower-confidence (the dashed tails, the hollow dots). They share the demand; the precise emphasis is still to be confirmed. The piece argues they share the demand, not that they are interchangeable.

Method. InfraWatch, developed by CIPE, with the NPV toolkit led by IEA-Kenya and disclosure mapping to the CoST IDS. The tools named per lane (NPV, PEA, IDS, beneficial ownership) are the existing instruments each priority maps to.
