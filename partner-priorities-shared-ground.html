Day 1 in Nairobi opened with five partners around one table: Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria, Ethiopia, DRC. Five countries, five organizations, and what looked at first like five separate agendas.

Five partners asked for five different things. It was the same sentence.

Read their asks as they were said, then watch the words fall away. Underneath the local variation, every partner was making one demand.

Day-1 morning · five asks, as they were said

Five asks

Five countries, five priorities.

Kenya wants the appraisal before the build. Ghana, the owner of the resource. Nigeria, the numbers. Ethiopia, the quality of the investment. DRC, the delivery. Five different priorities, in five partners' own words. Ethiopia and DRC are lower-confidence readings, still to be confirmed with each partner.

One demand

Strip the words, and it was one sentence.

Every ask begins the same way. The stem is identical, Show us; only the tail changes. That shared stem is the common ground: do not ask citizens to trust what they cannot see. The tails are the legitimate local variation, not five competing agendas.

One name

The language to act on it already exists.

None of these needs inventing. Proven tools already name each tail, disclosure standards, political-economy analysis, NPV, beneficial-ownership data, so a shared push can skip the fight over definitions and go straight to the ask.

One number

So each ask resolves to one number.

A demand a government can dismiss. A single figure it cannot. Each tail becomes one measurable number, the thing that would prove the change actually moved. The next question for the room is whether each partner can name theirs.

How we read this, and what stays uncertain

What this is. A working read of the Day-1 morning session in Nairobi, 2 June 2026, not a completed partner survey. Each partner's ask is captured in their own framing; the priorities grid is an analyst's reading of which themes each partner emphasised, to be confirmed with each partner before any external use.

What stays uncertain. Ethiopia and DRC are tagged as lower-confidence readings (the dashed tails, and the hollow dots in the grid). They share the demand; the precise emphasis is still to be confirmed. The point of the piece is that the five share the demand, not that they are interchangeable.

Method. InfraWatch, developed by CIPE, with the NPV toolkit led by IEA-Kenya and disclosure mapping to the CoST IDS. The "proven tools" named per ask (NPV, PEA, IDS, beneficial ownership) are the existing instruments each priority maps to, no new method required.
