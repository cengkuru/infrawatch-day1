The PPP questions · what a PPP must additionally disclose

A PPP is scored on eight extra disclosure themes, because its costs hide outside the budget

A project that meets the PPP definition is scored on the standard project information plus the questions below. They exist because the standard data points cannot see a PPP's hidden obligations: the guarantees, contingent liabilities and long payment promises that never appear in a budget line.

The assessment tests whether the evidence was published, never whether the deal was good. A PPP with terrible value for money and full disclosure scores high; a good deal nobody can verify scores low. Do not let a view of the project leak into the score of its record.

The eight themes

For each theme the question is the same: has the government published this evidence, and how easy is it to reach on the 0 to 6 scale?

1Rationale and value for moneyWhy a PPP was chosen over public procurement, and the published appraisal or value-for-money evidence behind that choice.
2Risk allocationWhich risks the private partner carries and which stay with government, as recorded in the contract or a published risk matrix.
3Ownership and beneficial ownershipThe project company's structure and its ultimate owners, published, not inferred.
4Guarantees and contingent liabilitiesWhat government has promised to pay if things go wrong: guarantees, minimum-revenue commitments, termination compensation exposure.
5Tariffs and payment mechanismsWhat users or government pay, how it is set, and how it changes over the contract's life.
6Performance and penaltiesThe service standards the partner must meet, and what happens when it misses them.
7RenegotiationsAny changes to the deal after signature: what changed, why, and at what cost.
8Termination and handoverHow the contract ends, what government pays or receives, and the condition the asset returns in.

How the themes are tiered: Tier 1 is what partners verify first

The partner summary groups the module into tiers. Tier 1 is the four evidence families a partner verifies on every genuine PPP: appraisal and decision evidence; risk allocation with its guarantees; funding sources, instruments and control; and beneficial ownership. They draw on themes 1 to 4 above plus the project's financing record. The remaining themes, tariffs, performance, renegotiations, termination and handover, follow in later tiers whose operational detail lives in the assessment workbook. Four families and eight themes are the same module at two levels of grouping, not two question sets.

What happens next

If the project is a PPP: score the standard points and these themes. If a document is missing: the contract that would answer a theme is itself a PPP disclosure record, so its absence is unverified or a searched-and-absent zero, never an exemption. If the project failed the PPP definition: these questions do not apply and leave the calculation entirely.
