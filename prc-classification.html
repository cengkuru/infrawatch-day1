PRC and non-PRC projects · the classification

The financier's origin decides, not the builder's flag

The programme compares how transparency behaves under financing from the People's Republic of China and financing from elsewhere. That comparison only works if the label is applied mechanically: by where the qualifying money ultimately comes from, and by nothing else.

The test

Start from the financing that qualified the project for the dataset, and trace it to its ultimate origin. The red case is the common error: attributing a project by who built it rather than by who financed it.

What each label means

Both labels come from the same question. Neither is a verdict on the project.

A PRC project

Qualifying financing whose ultimate origin is the People's Republic of China

  • The sources that count: a Chinese state policy or commercial bank holding the loan, a Chinese state-owned enterprise or state-linked fund holding equity, or a Chinese private company holding a qualifying stake.
  • Trace to origin. A stake held through a third-country holding vehicle counts by its ultimate owner, exactly as the shared definition requires.
  • Record state linkage separately. Whether the source is a state bank, a state enterprise or a private company does not change the country label, but it is recorded, because the disclosure questions differ.
A non-PRC project

The same test, with the origin anywhere else

  • Attributed to its own country, not to a residual category: a project financed from Japan is a Japan project, one financed from Germany is a Germany project.
  • Held to the same evidence standard. The financing must be genuine, signed and documented, exactly as for a PRC project. A weaker standard on one side would corrupt the comparison.
  • Includes public capital. A project financed by another government directly, or government to government, classifies by that government's country.
What never decides the label
  • The builder. A construction contract is not financing; the contractor's nationality never enters the test. This is the most common misclassification.
  • Suppliers, equipment, consultants and workers. Services sold to a project carry no financing origin.
  • The currency of the loan or payment. Money is classified by its source, not its denomination.
  • Where the holding company is registered. Registration is a mailbox; origin is traced through it.

The working rules

  1. Classify by the qualifying financing. The financing that brought the project into the dataset under the eligibility test is the financing that sets the label.
  2. Mixed financing: record everything, flag the project. Where qualifying financing comes from more than one country, record every financier, classify by the largest documented share, and flag the project as mixed. practice being confirmed
  3. Concentration is a finding, not a flaw. A list dominated by one financier country reflects the financing landscape of that country and is itself part of the evidence.
  4. The label carries no verdict. A PRC project is not presumed less transparent, and a non-PRC project is not presumed more. The assessment scores published records, project by project; the classification only makes the comparison possible.
