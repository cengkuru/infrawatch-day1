Public-value proof test

Can the public prove the railway was worth it?

Do not start with a file. Start with a claim: public money built public value. Then try to prove it using only the record a citizen can inspect.

Step 1

The public record can describe the project.

You can usually tell what was built, where it runs, which institutions were involved, and that public finance sits underneath it. That is useful, but it is not a value-for-money proof.

A description answers "what is this?" It does not answer "should this have been built on these terms?"

Step 2

The calculation breaks when cost becomes real.

A project cost is not only a headline capital amount. The public needs the price of borrowing, repayment timing, fees, and guarantee exposure. Those records determine who carries the bill when the optimistic version fails.

This is where the story stops being about "transparency" in general and becomes about loan terms and fiscal risk.

Step 3

The value claim breaks at the appraisal.

Even if the route is visible and the railway is operating, the public cannot judge whether the decision was justified without the expected benefits, alternatives, assumptions, and baseline used before approval.

Without the appraisal, actual performance has no public benchmark. The record can report activity, but not prove worth.

Step 4

The stronger ask is not "more openness." It is the missing proof.

The public-value test produces a sharper disclosure demand: publish the records that let citizens calculate cost, identify risk, and compare expected benefits with actual results.

That is the narrative turn: secrecy matters most where it prevents judgment, not where it merely leaves a blank page.

The Day 1 finding

Transparency runs out exactly where judgment begins.

The public can identify the SGR. It cannot complete the proof that the investment was priced well, risked responsibly, and chosen over better alternatives.

Cost proof

Publish the financing terms.

Interest rate, fees, maturity, grace period, repayment schedule, and currency exposure.

Risk proof

Publish the guarantee exposure.

The instrument, trigger conditions, contingent liability treatment, and who carries downside risk.

Value proof

Publish the appraisal.

Feasibility study, cost-benefit analysis, expected benefits, assumptions, and alternatives.

Reality check

Compare promises to actuals.

Ridership, revenue, operating cost, maintenance burden, and public subsidy against the original baseline.
