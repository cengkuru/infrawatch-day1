You can photograph the Standard Gauge Railway. You can ride it, count its trains, read its opening-day headlines. But ask the two questions that actually decide whether it was a good idea, and the record goes black. The easy questions are public. The ones that matter are locked.

The deeper you dig, the darker it gets.

Five questions about one railway. The first three are on the record. The last two, what it really cost and whether it was worth it, are exactly the ones you cannot see.

dig in ↓

five questions, top to bottom

start at the surface

What is it?

public

A railway from Mombasa to Nairobi. Stations, trains, ridership: announced, opened, photographed. The surface of the project is fully on the record.

one layer down

How is it paid for?

public

A sovereign loan: a foreign export-import bank lends, and the state borrows and guarantees. Still visible, still on the record.

deeper

Who decides?

public

The treasury, the railway, and the lender. Notice already: the people with the power and the people who carry the cost barely overlap. This is the last layer you can see clearly.

the record goes black

What does it really cost?

🔒 locked

The interest rate, the repayment schedule, the sovereign guarantee on the full amount. This is the question a citizen most needs answered, and it is sealed.

unlocked by: loan terms & guarantee (CoST IDS · Prep + Impl)

the deepest, the darkest

Was it worth it?

🔒 locked

The cost-benefit analysis and feasibility study behind the decision to build, projected against what actually happened. The single most important question, and the most completely hidden.

unlocked by: the appraisal (CoST IDS · Preparation)

The Day 1 finding, in one breath

Transparency is not missing at random. It runs out exactly where it matters most. The questions you can answer about the SGR are the easy ones. The two that decide whether it was a good idea, what it cost and whether it was worth it, are the two that are locked.
