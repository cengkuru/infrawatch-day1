The project result · how to calculate one project's score

Add the scores that apply, then divide by the number of points you scored

This page shows how a project's 40-point disclosure checklist becomes a single number. The number answers one question: how easy is it to find the official project information? The hard part is not the division; it is deciding which points belong in the calculation.

The rule that decides everything: missing information scores zero only when it should already exist. Information that is not yet due, or does not apply to this project, is left out of the calculation entirely. Scoring a not-yet-due point as zero "to be safe" punishes a government for a record that cannot exist yet.

Step 1: should this point be scored?

Ask it for each of the 40 points. This decision, not the arithmetic, is the work.

Step 2: count, add, divide

A road project still in early works: seven of its 40 points should not be scored yet.

Sum of the 33 scores, including every due-but-unpublished zero99

99 total ÷ 33 points scored3.0

The 33 is the number you divide by; in the methodology this is called the denominator. Every point counts equally.

Project result
3.0 out of 6

The public record is moderately findable, with gaps. It says nothing about whether the road is a good road.

What the number does and does not tell you

It tells you

How findable and complete the official project information is, across the points that should exist right now.

It does not tell you
  • Whether the project is good or bad
  • Whether it is well managed
  • Whether the spending is value for money
  • Whether one project matters more than another

Two projects can share a 3.0 with entirely different failures underneath; the forty answers, not the average, are where the analysis reads. A 5.1 is not a good project and a 1.4 is not a bad one.

Projects with more than one awarded contract

For now, calculate and report each awarded contract separately. Do not combine the results. Whole-project documents, like the feasibility study, count toward every contract. CIPE will confirm how contract results combine into one project result in Phase 2.
Method note for reviewers
  • Equal weight per point is the working interpretation unless CIPE specifies otherwise; a phase with more applicable points therefore carries more of the average.
  • The profile stays the finding. The result is a summary statistic over the project's score profile; it summarises the forty answers and never replaces them.
  • Exclusions ride the evidence states. Not-yet-due and not-applicable points leave the calculation via their recorded evidence states; only a due, applicable, sufficiently searched and unpublished point is a genuine zero.
  • Per-contract scoring and the future roll-up are the same decision seen from two directions and will be settled together in Phase 2, with partner-specific scenarios; see also the project unit.
