Public-private partnership · the unified definition

A partnership is defined by risk and time, not by its label

Every country's law defines a public-private partnership differently, and some do not define it at all. So the programme applies one definition everywhere, the same move as with foreign financing: six tests, and an arrangement is a PPP only when all six hold, whatever the contract calls itself.

The six tests

These reproduce the screening questions in the partner assessment workbook. Run them on the arrangement as it actually operates, not on its title.

Test 1Is there a public authority on one side and a private partner on the other?A government body or state-linked company as the public party, and a private company as the partner. Two public bodies contracting each other is government to government, not a PPP.
Test 2Is the relationship long-term and contractual, rather than short-term or ad hoc?Concessions and purchase agreements run for decades. A construction contract that ends at handover fails this test.
Test 3Does the private partner contribute to financing, development, operation or maintenance?The partner puts something of its own into the asset's life, beyond being paid for a delivered service.
Test 4Does the private partner carry significant management responsibility?It runs something: the asset, the service, the revenue collection. A passive lender fails this test.
Test 5Is the private partner exposed to substantial project risk?Demand, performance or cost risk that actually lands on the partner. Where the state absorbs every downside, there is no partnership, only procurement with extra steps.
Test 6Is payment tied to long-term performance, availability or use?The partner earns over time from tolls, tariffs, availability payments or output, not from a lump sum at delivery.
All six holdIt is a PPP for assessment purposes: score the standard 40 data points plus the PPP questions (rationale and value for money, risk allocation, ownership and beneficial-ownership structures, guarantees and contingent liabilities, tariffs and payment mechanisms, performance and penalties, renegotiations, termination and handover).
Any test failsIt is not a PPP for assessment purposes, whatever its title says. Score the standard data points that apply. Record which test failed; the reason is often itself informative.

The rules that keep it comparable

  • The public contracting authority stays the project owner. Even where a special-purpose vehicle finances, builds, operates or temporarily holds title, the public entity that approved the project and answers for the service remains the owner. Otherwise "owner" would mean different things across projects and comparability dies.
  • The family is bigger than the name. Concessions, build-operate-transfer structures and independent power producers under a power purchase agreement all sit in this family when the six tests hold.
  • PPP status and foreign financing are separate questions. The six tests say nothing about where the money comes from; that is settled by the shared foreign-financing definition. A PPP can be entirely domestic, and a foreign-financed project need not be a PPP.
  • Why the extra questions exist: the standard data points cannot see a PPP's hidden obligations, the guarantees, contingent liabilities and long payment promises that never appear in a budget line. The PPP questions exist to reach them.
