Responsible public authority · whose record is scored

Score the entity that answers to the public, never the one that paid or built

Scoring measures one government entity's disclosure about its project. So before any point is scored, every project names its responsible public authority: the body a citizen could hold answerable if the project failed. Everyone else in the delivery chain is recorded by role, never scored.

The operational definition

One of the five controlled terms the mapping report's Annex B fixes for assessors.

The responsible public authority is the public entity with ultimate responsibility for initiating, overseeing and being answerable for the project. It is identified by responsibility, never by who paid, built, operated or hosts the website.

Infra Watch mapping report, Annex B, operational terms

Finding it in a crowded delivery chain

A real project's chain can hold a PPP unit, a line ministry, a regulator, a state enterprise, a project company and several lenders. The report's Annex E lists each partner country's chain; the assessor's job is to find the public authority inside it.

  1. Strip out everyone who can never be scored. The private partner, the project company or SPV, and the lenders appear in every chain. They are recorded by role; they are never the entity scored.
  2. Where several public bodies remain, the one with primary formal responsibility for delivery, or for the concession award, is the entity scored. The others are recorded by role.
  3. Where state linkage is debatable, the deciding question stays responsibility: which entity answers to the public if the project fails.
The caseThe ruling
A foreign state-owned enterprise holds the concession and operates the assetThe granting public authority is the project owner and the entity scored. The concessionaire is recorded as the private partner.
An SPV finances, builds and temporarily holds titleThe public contracting authority stays the owner; the SPV is a recorded field, never the scored entity.
A funder's website carries the best project pageHosting decides nothing. The authority is identified by responsibility, and funder-only publication caps at 2 on the scale.
A state-owned enterprise implements a sovereign-loan projectIdentify the public body answerable for the project; the SOE's role in the contract is classified separately.

The owner and the discloser are often different bodies

In most partner countries the legal duty to publish sits with a PPP unit, department or agency even where a line ministry owns the project.

That gap is a finding, not an error. Where the entity that owns a project and the entity obliged to disclose it are different, the assessment still scores the responsible public authority's record, and the gap between owner and discloser is recorded as an institutional finding in its own right. It is one of the things the programme exists to make visible.
The common trap: scoring whoever published. A ministry with a weak website is not rescued by its regulator's good one, and a project is not penalised because its documents sit on the PPP unit's portal rather than the ministry's. The authority is fixed first, by responsibility; then the search asks what the government, through its channels, made findable about that authority's project.
