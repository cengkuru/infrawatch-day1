Ten rules and assumptions behind the split

  1. The draw is unchanged

    Draw IW-DQA-2026-11-09 on Jolo's list of 194 scored projects, re-run on 9 Nov, gives the same 41 projects (list fingerprint 7c1ff8bc): one in five per country, rounded up, with PPPs drawn in proportion. Only who reviews what has changed.

  2. No country reviews its own work

    IDEAS reviews nothing from Malaysia, IEA Kenya nothing from Kenya and Verité nothing from Sri Lanka.

  3. IDEAS takes exactly 8

    IDEAS scored 12 projects and its contract is for 20, so it reviews 8: Indonesia 4 and Sri Lanka 4.

  4. Verité takes the countries it asked for

    Cambodia, the Philippines and Nepal, 12 projects. None of the 12 is a PPP.

  5. IEA Kenya takes 14

    Without Kenya, the African projects in the draw come to 13 (Ethiopia 5, Ghana 4, Nigeria 4). The 14th is one Malaysian project, ECRL (Malaysia 5), picked by a seeded shuffle of the two Malaysian projects that are not PPPs, so anyone can repeat the choice.

  6. CIPE takes 7, across both regions

    Kenya 5 and Malaysia 2 (Pulau Indah PPP and the Second Penang Bridge). Five reviewers share them: Jolo 3, Ria 1, and Alissa, Srujana and Jeremy one each. Alissa reviews Africa only, so the Malaysian two go to Jolo. Jolo assigns the Kenyan five within CIPE.

  7. PPPs go where the countries go

    The three PPPs in the draw sit with CIPE (Kenya OrPower, Malaysia Pulau Indah) and IEA Kenya (Nigeria Dadin Kowa). The earlier rule that IDEAS reviews PPPs only no longer applies.

  8. Earlier splits are replaced

    This replaces the split in the 9 Nov workbook (IEA Kenya 14, Verité 12, Jolo 11, IDEAS 2, Ria 2). Projects that were also in the 2 Nov draw no longer keep their earlier reviewer.

  9. PPP flags follow Jolo's list

    Where partners mark more projects as PPPs (Malaysia, Nepal, Ghana), the draw keeps Jolo's list and records the difference as a finding. If CIPE prefers the partners' flags, the Malaysian and Nepali picks change but the number per country, and so this split, does not.

  10. Three weeks from access

    Each reviewer has three weeks from the day access is granted. Access goes per workbook, so a reviewer given a country workbook also sees the other sheets in it.
