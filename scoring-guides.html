Scoring guides · which ruling settles this call?

Every standing ruling of the assessment, one line each

Twenty-four rulings answer the judgment calls that recur in scoring. Five carry most of the weight: score the government, not whoever runs the website; score only what can exist at the project's stage; every score needs one accountable public owner; every figure carries its source, date and currency; convenience never picks the sample or the answer. Each line links to the page that carries the full rule.

Never apply a Pending ruling as if it were settled. Rulings 19, 24 and 25 are awaiting a CIPE or Verité decision; apply the current draft, mark the record, and flag it.

Which projects enter the study

4Eligibility reads control and stake, never the instrument nameWorkingPublic infrastructure, material foreign stake, foreign ultimate control identified. Loan, BOT or equity labels do not decide. Full rule: eligibility test.
13All three scope answers must be yesWorkingA partial yes produces an unbounded study. A domestic-only concession fails question two and stays out. Full rule: eligibility test.
14No government owner, no transparency scoreWorkingWhere nobody public answers for a project, the row is context, never scored. Ownership is tested in documents, never by intuition. Full rule: responsible public authority.
24Selection must not depend on data availabilityPendingEasy-to-find projects make disclosure look better than it is; a hard-to-document project belongs in the sample precisely so it can score low. Options with CIPE. Full rule: selection and sampling.

Who gets scored

3Under a concession, the owner is the granting public authorityAgreedNever the private concessionaire or operator. Keeps PPPs in scope without scoring private companies. Full rule: responsible public authority.
15Several entities involved: score the one with primary formal responsibilityAgreedEstablished from documents. The contracting authority named in the agreement, not the line ministry above it. Full rule: responsible public authority.
17The owner is scored; a mandate-holder may publishAgreedA PPP unit's portal entry scores for the contracting authority's project; the mandated channel earns full credit. Full rule: qualifying government publication.

Whose disclosure a source is

1Who controls the publication decides the sourceWorkingA legally mandated national database is the government speaking, wherever it is hosted. Full rule: qualifying government publication.
2Funder-only publication caps the government score at 2WorkingThe evidence stays, labelled. Crediting it would measure funder publishing culture, not government transparency. Full rule: qualifying government publication.
23Authorship decides; hosting never doesAgreedA ministry page linking a Drive-hosted signed contract can reach 6; information needing a request or contact fails accessibility. Full rule: qualifying government publication.
25Oversight publications score only within the oversight mandatePendingA published audit IS the audit point's disclosure; a contract price known only from that audit is recorded, labelled, and not scored. Proposed with Verité. Full rule: oversight publication.

How points are awarded

6An empty cell has four meanings; only one is a zeroWorkingNot disclosed (due, searched, not found) is the only failure. Not yet due, structurally absent and out of scope are labels. A published "none" is disclosure. Full rule: evidence states.
8No project is scored on points its stage has not reachedWorkingA plant commissioning in 2029 is not scored on completion records; impossible documents cannot be failures. Full rule: lifecycle and due date.
12Where the usual record does not exist, the expectation movesWorkingA direct-award concession is scored on its award justification and negotiation record, not a tender dossier. Structural absence is documented, never asserted. Full rule: lifecycle and due date.
19Stage and point type decide applicabilityPendingEntering construction makes earlier-stage records due; trigger-based points (resettlement, overruns) stay conditional at every stage. CIPE confirmation pending. Full rule: lifecycle and due date.
22Phased projects are scored per awarded contractAgreedThree phases, three awards: three scores. A corridor-wide EIA counts for every phase it explicitly covers. Full rule: project unit.
5A delivery model is not a financing arrangementAgreedEPC turnkey and PPP say how a project is delivered, never where the money came from; keep the two classifications separate. Full rule: financing forms.

What counts as a fact

9Every figure carries its source, date, currency and rateWorkingA number without them is not disclosed evidence, including our own working figures. Full rule: data collection.
16Multiple published figures: label every scope, elect noneWorkingAn approved budget and a revised contract price may both be true; keep each with its publisher, scope and date, and flag the conflict. Full rule: conflicting figures.
21Comparable government figures conflict: the direct line agency governsAgreedAfter confirming they measure the same thing, the contracting authority's signed contract governs; star the divergence, it is a finding. Full rule: conflicting figures.

Reporting and process

7"Not assessable" is a submission status, never a scoreWorkingMaterial that missed the cutoff is reported as pending with what is needed stated, never scored zero, never compared against completed scores. Full rule: assessment process.
10Coverage counts use the confirmed denominatorAgreedAny "X of N countries" claim uses the registry's confirmed count, denominator stated. Ten mapped countries; the definition-source partner is not an eleventh.
18A submission counts from when it was sentAgreedSent the day before the cutoff and read three days later met the cutoff. Deadlines govern partner behaviour, not reviewers' inboxes. Full rule: validation sheet.
11Score first, characterise secondWorkingExpected scores and transparency labels never travel inside scoring packs; a deck predicting "least transparent" is set aside until scoring is complete. Full rule: assessment process.
If a ruling settles your call: apply it and record which one. If two rulings collide, or none fits: do not improvise a local rule; take it to referral and resolution.
