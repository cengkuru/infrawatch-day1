Selection and sampling · building the country list

Choose projects before you see their disclosure record

First decide which projects qualify. Only then look at how much information has been published. Never choose or remove a project because its information is easy or difficult to find.

There is no single sampling method that works in every country. Your approach must be clear, consistently applied and documented before scoring begins; this page shows you how.

The selection workflow, start to finish

The one rule that protects everything: never select a project because its information is easy to find. The assessment measures how findable the record is; choose projects for their good records and the sample flatters the system it was meant to test. If a data-availability screen is ever unavoidable, list the projects it excluded, so the bias is visible instead of silent.

Each step, in plain terms

StepWhat you do, and what you produce
1. Make the longlistSearch the main official project sources available in your country: the public investment programme, the budget, the procurement portal, the PPP pipeline, development-partner project lists, sector-agency registers. Record the source of every project. You do not need every project in the country; you need every source a qualifying project could plausibly appear in.
2. Apply eligibilityFor every longlisted project, record included, or excluded with the reason, using the eligibility test.
3. Decide how manySee the three routes below. State your route and apply it consistently.
4. Check the balanceCheck whether your list is heavily concentrated in one sector, region, agency, financing source or project stage. You do not always need to correct the concentration, but you must record it; concentration that reflects the eligible pool is a finding, not a failure. Do not manufacture diversity.
5. Lock the listRecord the selected projects, the buffer projects, the selection method, and known limitations. If a chosen project later fails scrutiny, the replacement comes from the buffer, never a fresh pick. Do this before scoring begins.

Step 3 in detail: which route fits your list?

Your situationUse this approach
The eligible list is small enough to assess in fullInclude all eligible projects.
The eligible list is large and variedSelect projects across the important categories, preserving the main patterns in the longlist (stratified).
Particular projects matter to the studySelect them deliberately against stated criteria, and document why (purposive).
Is random sampling required? No. The aim is a credible, reviewable selection of eligible projects, not a statistically representative national survey. Whatever the route: do not select on disclosure availability, apply your stated rules consistently, and disclose the limits of the sample.

Worked example: 38 eligible, 20 selected, 5 in the buffer

  1. Longlist. A partner identifies 38 eligible projects from the national investment plan, the PPP pipeline and two sector registers, recording the source of each.
  2. Route. The study needs 20 projects, so the partner takes the large-list route: it groups the 38 by sector and financing source.
  3. Select. It picks 20 while preserving the main patterns of the longlist. Three candidates have very thin public records; they stay in, because difficulty of finding information is never a reason to remove a project.
  4. Buffer and lock. Five further eligible projects are placed on the buffer list, and the whole set, with method and limitations, is recorded before anyone scores anything.

Can recent eligible projects fill the sample? ladder being confirmed with CIPE

If yes, stop: use them. If not, widen the age window one step at a time, and record at each step why no more recent substitute existed. The flag on an aged project is not a penalty; it is what lets an older, well-documented project stand in a defensible list.

PreferredUnder construction or recently completed.
Step backUp to 3 years since completion.
ThenUp to 5 years, each admission documented.
Outer limitUp to 10 years, flagged. Never beyond.

Optional filters: what each one costs

Each filter is defensible when it is stated, applied uniformly, and its exclusion is named in the selection note. How much bias a filter carries depends on your country's systems; record the trade-off rather than assuming it.

FilterMain information lostWhat to watch
Recent projects onlyOlder disclosure patternsWhere procurement or publication systems changed recently, recency alone can flatter or damn the system; say which.
Minimum-value thresholdSmaller projects, which often escape scrutiny precisely because nobody is watchingLarge projects are usually better documented, so a size floor quietly readmits the availability bias. Name that consequence.
Completed projects onlyIn-construction transparencyCompleted-only also tilts the sample older, interacting with the age ladder; allows completion-stage evidence to be scored.

What is settled, recommended, and still open

Apply now
  • Never select on disclosure availability.
  • Apply your stated rules consistently.
  • Document included and excluded projects, with reasons.
Recommended approach
  • Build the longlist from named official sources.
  • Pre-commit a buffer before scoring.
  • Check concentration across sector, location, agency, financing source and stage.
Awaiting CIPE confirmation
  • The exact project-age ladder.
  • Any cohort-wide minimum or maximum age.
