Selection and sampling · building the country list

Choose projects before you see their disclosure record

Build a list from eligible projects, then assess how transparent they are. Do not select projects because their information is easy to find.

There is no single sampling method that works in every country. Your approach may be purposive, stratified or another practical method, but it must be clear, consistently applied and documented before scoring begins.

Build the country list in four steps

1
Build the pool

List potentially eligible projects from budgets, investment plans, PPP pipelines, debt and guarantee records, ODA lists, SOE and regulator registers. The pool is the record of what you chose from; keep it.

2
Apply the eligibility test

Keep only projects that meet the common financing, timing and implementation rules on the eligibility page, plus any stated filters (size floor, age window, completion status). Record what each filter excluded.

3
Choose a balanced sample

Seek a reasonable spread across financier country, sector, geography, project type or size where the country pool allows it. If one financier or sector genuinely dominates the pool, reflect that reality rather than manufacturing diversity: concentration is a finding about how infrastructure is financed, not a sampling failure.

4
Document the choice before scoring

Record the universe and its sources, the eligibility filter, the time window, the sampling approach, the desired and actual spread, the reason each project was chosen, and the limits on what the sample can represent. Someone else should be able to re-create your list from your notes.

The rule that protects the assessment

Never select a project because its information is easy to find. The assessment measures how findable the record is; choose projects for their good records and the scores stop measuring disclosure and start measuring your own choosing. A well-documented sample can make a weak disclosure system look stronger than it is. The same bias returns in softer clothes as "projects with adequate data available": if a data-availability screen is ever unavoidable, list the projects it excluded, so the bias is visible instead of silent.

Is random sampling required?

No. The aim is not a statistically random national survey; it is a credible, reviewable selection of eligible projects. Random selection is not required, and may not be practical where eligibility and comparability substantially narrow the available pool. Partners may use purposive or stratified sampling where that better reflects the country's project universe. The safeguards are shared whatever the method: do not select on disclosure availability, apply your stated rules consistently, and disclose the limits of the sample.

Age of projects: the working ladder being confirmed with CIPE

Prefer recent projects; where they cannot fill the list, widen in stages and record at each step why no more recent substitute existed. The flag on an aged project is not a penalty; it is what lets an older, well-documented project stand in a defensible list.

PreferredUnder construction or recently completed.
Step backUp to 3 years since completion.
ThenUp to 5 years, each admission documented.
Outer limitUp to 10 years, flagged. Never beyond.

Choices that change what your sample can represent

Each filter is defensible when it is stated, applied uniformly, and its exclusion is named in the documentation.

ChoiceWhy use itWhat it excludes
Recent or completed projectsEnables a fuller lifecycle review, including completion records where trails most often thinOlder projects and live-project disclosure patterns
Minimum-value thresholdKeeps the work feasibleSmaller projects, which often escape scrutiny precisely because nobody is watching. Large projects are also usually better documented, so a size floor quietly readmits the availability bias; name that consequence
Completed-only sampleAllows completion-stage evidence to be scoredIn-construction transparency

Where this connects

Only projects passing the eligibility test enter the pool. If a chosen project later fails scrutiny, its replacement comes from the pre-committed buffer list, never from a fresh convenience pick. As each project enters the list, record its financing form and classification.
