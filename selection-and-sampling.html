Selection and sampling · building the country list

Choose projects before you see their disclosure record

Build a list from eligible projects, then assess how transparent they are. Do not select projects because their information is easy to find.

There is no single sampling method that works in every country. Your approach may be purposive, stratified or another practical method, but it must be clear, consistently applied and documented before scoring begins.

The selection workflow, start to finish

What each step produces

StepWhat the partner produces
Build the poolA named candidate universe and its sources
Apply eligibilityAn included and excluded list, with reasons
Choose a methodA short statement of the sampling approach
Check representationA note on the desired and actual spread; concentration recorded as a finding, not a failure
Lock the listCore sample, pre-committed buffer, and the selection note. If a chosen project later fails scrutiny, the replacement comes from the buffer, never a fresh pick
The one rule that protects everything: never select a project because its information is easy to find. The assessment measures how findable the record is; choose projects for their good records and the sample flatters the system it was meant to test. A well-documented sample can make a weak disclosure system look stronger than it is. If a data-availability screen is ever unavoidable, list the projects it excluded, so the bias is visible instead of silent.
Is random sampling required? No. The aim is a credible, reviewable selection of eligible projects, not a statistically random national survey. Random selection may not be practical where eligibility narrows the pool. The safeguards are shared whatever the method: do not select on disclosure availability, apply your stated rules consistently, and disclose the limits of the sample.

Can recent eligible projects fill the sample? ladder being confirmed with CIPE

If yes, stop: use them. If not, widen the age window one step at a time, and record at each step why no more recent substitute existed. The flag on an aged project is not a penalty; it is what lets an older, well-documented project stand in a defensible list.

PreferredUnder construction or recently completed.
Step backUp to 3 years since completion.
ThenUp to 5 years, each admission documented.
Outer limitUp to 10 years, flagged. Never beyond.

Optional filters, and the bias each one carries

Each filter is defensible when it is stated, applied uniformly, and its exclusion is named in the selection note.

FilterEffect on the sampleBias risk
Recent projects onlyExcludes older disclosure patternsLow
Minimum-value thresholdExcludes smaller projects, which often escape scrutiny precisely because nobody is watchingHigh — large projects are better documented, so a size floor quietly readmits the availability bias
Completed projects onlyExcludes in-construction transparency; allows full-lifecycle scoringMedium

A partner who follows this workflow produces a sample that is credible without pretending every country has the same project universe or the same feasible sampling method.
