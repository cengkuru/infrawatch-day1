Sufficient search · the floor under every zero

An unsearched point is never a zero

A sufficient search is a completed search of the government channels where this data point could reasonably be published, with the search itself recorded. A zero claims the government failed to publish, and that claim is only as strong as the search behind it: zero is not "I did not find it"; zero is "I searched the right official places, recorded the search, and the due fact was not published."

Before recording zero, confirm all four

The stopping condition
  1. The data point applies to this project and is already due.
  2. You searched every government channel where this fact could reasonably appear.
  3. You searched using the project's relevant names and languages.
  4. You recorded where, when and what you searched.

If any one is missing, the point is unsearched, not zero.

The two traps that produce false zeros: stopping when something is found, and the quick look. Finding the budget on the funder's project page answers a different question; the assessment measures what the government made findable, so the search ends only when the government's own channels are covered. And one portal, one language, no record is a glance, not a search. Neither supports a zero; a zero without a search record is an insufficiently searched point, not a finding.

Which channels count as the government's

Search the channels where this data point would plausibly live in your country, not every site in the country. Your country's list is in the source inventory.

Search here to test government disclosure

  • The PPP unit or PPP disclosure portal, where one exists.
  • The procurement portal for tender and award points.
  • Debt, budget and aid databases for financing points.
  • The responsible ministry's own pages, gazettes and parliamentary records.

Search here for context, but it is not government disclosure

  • Funder project pages and lender-side databases.
  • Third-party trackers and research databases.
  • Press coverage quoting official figures.
  • Keep what these find, labelled; it is not government publication and caps at 2 on the scale.

What to write down

Record where you looked, when, what you searched for, and what you found or did not find, so someone else can retrace your steps: channel names and addresses, the date of each search, the identifiers and host-language terms used, and the source, publisher and date of anything found. Addresses and holdings change, so the date is part of the claim.
Worked example: where no consolidated portal exists

A budget point for a Cambodian expressway concession, in a country the report notes has no consolidated portal:

  1. Start from the inventory. Cambodia's listed channels: the MEF General Department of PPP site, and the MEF public debt statistical bulletins. No consolidated tender portal is identified.
  2. Search each channel for the project by name and by concession, in English and Khmer, and record each search with its date.
  3. Extend to the responsible authority. The contracting institution's own pages and any gazette or official announcement channel it uses.
  4. Record the funder's figures separately. The concession is documented mainly by its financiers and the press; that evidence is kept, labelled, and capped at 2. It does not end the search for the government record.
  5. Close the search. Nothing qualifying found: the point is due, the search is complete and recorded, and only now does the field take a zero, recorded as due but unpublished with the search record as its evidence.
