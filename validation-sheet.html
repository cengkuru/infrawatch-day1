Validation sheet · how country responses enter the record

The validation sheet is the form a country team returns to confirm or correct what the assessment has recorded

It travels with the partner summary and the mapping report. Each country team tests the method’s rules against its own projects, corrects any project or regulatory record with a source, and returns the sheet by email. It is the instrument of partner validation: corrections enter the record through it, never as silent edits.

Never treat an unreturned sheet as agreement. Silence leaves the recorded facts standing as unconfirmed, and they are used with that status visible. Only a returned sheet moves a fact from recorded to validated.

The five sections, and what each one asks

One sheet per country team, one response per item. Objections travel with a named project and a source, never as opinion alone.

ADo the six core rules work in your country?One response per rule: works as intended, needs adjustment, or needs clearer wording, with a country example.
BDo the four CoST recommendations hold against your evidence?Government publication, which official figure takes precedence, project age, and multi-contract projects. A named country exception or factual correction needs a source.
CDoes the PPP treatment classify your projects correctly?Check the three-condition PPP test against selected projects, then run one real collection trial on a single PPP across four information areas.
D EWhich recorded facts need correcting?Correct any project or regulatory record with the correcting fact and its source, or mark the no-corrections boxes explicitly.

What counts as a completed return

It counts
  • Every row marked: confirmed, corrected with a source, or cannot confirm with the reason.
  • The respondent’s details block filled in, returned in Word format by email reply.
  • An explicit no-corrections mark where nothing needs changing.
It does not count
  • Unmarked rows: a partial return, not a completed validation.
  • A correction without a source: nothing to verify against.
  • An objection with no named project: opinion, not evidence.

Worked example: testing one rule against one country

  1. Starting facts. A partner finds a ministry-authored project document hosted only on a funder’s website.
  2. Rule applied. Section B, the government-publication recommendation: authorship is the operating test, wherever the document is hosted.
  3. Result. The partner marks “works in this context” or names the exception, citing the project and the source.
  4. Recorded. The response enters the review; what changes in the final method is documented and reported back.

What happens next

If returned: every response is recorded, the evidence is reviewed, and changes to the final method are documented and shown back to partners. If not returned: the recorded facts stand as unconfirmed and carry that status wherever they are used. If the evidence does not settle an item: mark “not enough evidence to test”; that is a recorded answer, not a gap.

Follow the feedback after it is returned

The live validation progress page shows what partner feedback confirms, changes or leaves open, along with the current treatment and decision owner. It keeps received feedback, CoST working positions, CIPE decisions and published changes as separate states.
